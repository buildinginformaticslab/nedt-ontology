Dear Editors of *Advanced Engineering Informatics*,

Please consider our manuscript, **“A Knowledge-Graph Framework for Transformer-Level Planning under Residential Technology-Adoption Scenarios,”** for publication in *Advanced Engineering Informatics*.

The manuscript addresses a knowledge-intensive engineering task: connecting heterogeneous residential building-stock evidence, technology-adoption scenarios, electricity-network assets, capacity indicators, and computational provenance for transformer-level electrification planning. Its contribution is an OWL 2 DL extension and a reproducible knowledge-graph workflow, rather than a standalone demand-modelling application. The Irish demonstration materialises scenario-specific capacity KPIs for 37,287 MV/LV catchments and 2.14 million dwellings. The semantic layer is evaluated through SHACL conformance, a HermiT consistency and transitive-roll-up regression test, and executed SPARQL competency questions against a 764,115-triple result graph.

The work aligns with AEI’s focus on explicit knowledge representation and its engineering use. It demonstrates how a reusable semantic model can support traceable, scenario-dependent infrastructure-exposure screening across otherwise disconnected building and electricity-network data. We deliberately distinguish this conditional screening function from prediction of observed transformer overload, and release the executable Scenario 1 workflow, RDF output, query results, manifest, and reconciliation audit needed to inspect that boundary.
